August 2024. Refused. I walked in underprepared: a reason I couldn't say in one clean sentence, and a stack of documents nobody opened. One question, and it was over. It was early morning, and I'm not a morning person. I'd flown in from Bangalore, so I couldn't even be sad in peace: there was a hotel to check out of and a flight home to catch. Somewhere over Andhra Pradesh, with a passport that had nothing new in it, the only thought was why?

February 2025. Approved. Same consulate, same window.

One interview means two visits to the consulate city: biometrics first, then the actual interview. Two attempts meant four trips to Hyderabad for me. This is what I changed in between.

What a former US visa officer told me

After the refusal I paid a former visa officer to go through my case, specifically: what the refusal meant for a founder like me, what to change, how to answer, and how to present my situation on the DS-160. His guidance, and why it worked, is in here.

If any of this sounds like you

  • You run something of your own, with or without a company registered yet.
  • You freelance or contract, and there's no salary slip or HR letter to show.
  • Your work doesn't fit the form: creator, consultant, a side project that became the main thing.
  • You've already been handed a 214(b) and you're wondering what to change.
  • You're applying for the first time and would rather not learn this the hard way.

I'm not a consultant and this isn't a system. It's what I wish someone had written down before my first attempt. One refusal cost me a lot more than this: the fee alone is around ₹16,000, non-refundable, before flights and a hotel, and doing all of it twice.
